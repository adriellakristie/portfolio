“What's near me,
What's on the way?”

The Problem

Finding food shouldn't mean going off your way.

When people are traveling and want to stop for food, existing map and food discovery tools primarily show restaurants based on proximity to a location. But being nearby doesn't necessarily mean being convenient. A restaurant might appear close on a map while requiring a significant detour from the user's actual route. This forces people to switch between searching for restaurants, checking directions and manually estimating how much time a stop will add to their journey.

The Opportunity

This revealed an opportunity to combine route planning and food discovery into one experience. Instead of making users compare restaurants and routes separately, OnTheWay could surface food options directly within the context of their journey.

The Research

How to achieve
convenience?

The problem isn't finding restaurants. It's understanding the trade-off.

I looked at how existing map and food discovery experiences help users find places to eat during a journey and found that most experiences follow a similar pattern:

Plan a route → Search for restaurants → Compare locations → Check directions → Estimate the detour

This creates unnecessary decision-making for the user. Distance alone doesn't communicate whether a restaurant is actually convenient. A key insight I gathered is that proximity ≠ convenience. The most useful information isn't simply how far away a restaurant is. It's how much stopping there will affect the journey. A restaurant that is slightly farther away may be directly along the route, while one that appears closer could require a significant detour.

The Solution

OnTheWay: Food discovery on your way

OnTheWay allows users to enter their starting point and destination, visualize their route, and discover restaurants along the way. Rather than simply showing restaurant locations, the experience highlights the detour cost of each option.
